What is HRV and Why Does It Matter for Meditation?

Heart Rate Variability (HRV) measures the variation in time between consecutive heartbeats. Contrary to popular belief, a healthy heart doesn't beat like a metronome — it constantly adjusts its rhythm in response to breathing, thoughts, and emotions.

During deep meditation, the parasympathetic nervous system (the "rest and digest" branch) becomes dominant. This shifts HRV into a coherent, sinusoidal pattern called respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA) — where heart rate rises with the in-breath and falls with the out-breath in a smooth wave.

Key Insight

HRV coherence is the physiological signature of meditation depth. When your mind is truly "one-pointed," your heart rhythm becomes coherent. ZENITY measures this coherence in real-time.

HRV in Buddhist Practice: One-Pointedness (Ekaggata)

In Buddhist meditation theory, ekaggata (one-pointedness) is the mental factor that unifies the mind on a single object — whether that's the breath, a Buddha-name, or a kasina (visualization object). The Visuddhimagga describes it as the "leader" of the five jhana factors.
